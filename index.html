1. Basic Principles

1.1 Using Complete Sentences

❌ Incorrect: Since \(x\) is positive. If this is not true. When two triangles are similar.
✅ Correct: Since \(x\) is positive, we have \(x+1 > 1\).
If this is not true, then the statement fails.
When two triangles are similar, their corresponding angles are equal.

1.2 Starting Sentences with Capital Letters

❌ \(p\) is not a prime number if it is divisible by 3 and greater than 3.
✅ If the number \(p\) is divisible by 3 and greater than 3, then it is not a prime number.

1.3 Commas between Variables

❌ In addition to \(p\) \(q\) is also a prime number.
✅ In addition to \(p\), the number \(q\) is also prime.

3. The Use of Symbols

3.1 The Equality Sign \(=\)

❌ derivative of \(x^2 = \frac{(x^2+2xh+h^2)-x^2}{h} = 2x\)
✅ derivative of \(x^2 = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{(x^2+2xh+h^2)-x^2}{h} = 2x\)

3.2 The Symbols \(\forall\) and \(\exists\)

❌ Wrong✅ Correct
\(\exists x \in \mathbb{R} \forall x > 1000\)\(\exists x \in \mathbb{R} \text{ s.t. } x > 1000\)
\(\forall x \in \mathbb{R} \text{ s.t. } x^2 \ge 0\)\(x^2 \ge 0 \; \forall x \in \mathbb{R}\)

4. Terminology by Topic

4.1 Mathematical Induction

❌ Assume \(S(k)\) is true for all positive integers \(k\).
✅ Assume \(S(k)\) is true for some positive integer \(k\).
❌ \(S(k+1) = 1 + 2 + \dots + (k+1)\)
✅ \(\text{LHS} = 1 + 2 + \dots + (k+1)\)
❌ By the principal of mathematical induction
✅ By the principle of mathematical induction

5. My Own Examples (Task 3c)

📌 Example 1: Describing Matrix Multiplication
Let \(A \in \mathbf{F}^{m \times n}\) and \(B \in \mathbf{F}^{n \times p}\).
Then the product \(AB \in \mathbf{F}^{m \times p}\) is defined by: \[ (AB)_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} A_{ik} B_{kj} \]
📌 Example 2: "e.g." vs "i.e."
❌ A prime number is e.g. 2, 3, 5.
✅ A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 with no positive divisors other than 1 and itself.
For example, 2, 3, and 5 are prime numbers.
✅ 2 is the unique even prime number, i.e. the only prime divisible by 2.
📌 Example 3: Logical Flow in Chain Equations
❌ Since \(x^4y^4/8 = 2\), we have \(x^4y^4 = (xy)^4 = 8 \times 2 = 16\).
✅ Since \(x^4y^4/8 = 2\), we have \(x^4y^4 = 16\).
Because \((xy)^4 = x^4y^4\), it follows that \((xy)^4 = 16\).

6. Handwriting Tips

  • \(t\) vs \(+\) — make the crossbar on \(t\) longer.
  • \(\ln\) vs \(\mathrm{In}\) — write 'ln' clearly, not 'In'.
  • \(x\) vs \(x\) — use cursive \(x\) to avoid confusion with ×.
  • \(a\) vs \(2\) — close the loop on \(a\).
  • \(0\) vs \(6\) vs \(\sigma\) — distinguish by context.
